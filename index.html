Why does turning it off and on again actually work?

Because software accumulates mess. Half-finished tasks, stuck connections, memory that never got handed back. Restarting throws all of it away and starts from a known state. It's not a magic trick, it's just the only guaranteed way back to a situation somebody has actually tested.

Should I really shut down my laptop every night?

Not every night, but not never either. Sleep mode is fine day to day. The reason to restart weekly is that most updates only finish applying on a reboot — so the person who never shuts down is often the person whose machine is quietly six updates behind.

Is my phone listening to me?

Almost certainly not in the way people mean. Constantly recording audio would demolish your battery and use noticeable data. The duller explanation is that advertising builds a startlingly good picture from what you search, where you go and who you're near. It feels like listening because the guess is so accurate.

What's the most common thing you fix?

Something being unplugged. I say that with total affection — it happens to everyone, including me, including in front of an audience. The second most common is a password with caps lock on.

Best tech advice for someone who isn't technical?

Back up your photos in two places, and turn on two-factor authentication for your email. Your email is the master key — whoever controls it can reset everything else. It's a boring answer, but I've never met anyone who regretted doing it.

Something about you that isn't technology?

I've lived on several continents and speak more than one language, which turns out to be more useful in IT than you'd expect — a lot of the job is translation of one kind or another. I swim, I go to the gym, and I play chess badly but enthusiastically.
